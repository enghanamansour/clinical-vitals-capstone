# ACVPU and NEWS2

The consciousness parameter uses the ACVPU scale: Alert scores 0; new Confusion,
or responding only to Voice, Pain, or being Unresponsive each score 3. New-onset
confusion is a common early sign of deterioration and is often missed if only a
GCS threshold is used. Any drop from Alert should be treated as a significant
change.

# Immediate assessment

Check a capillary blood glucose first in every patient with reduced
consciousness and treat hypoglycaemia immediately. Assess airway patency and
protect it if the patient cannot. Review the medication chart for opioids,
sedatives and anticholinergics. Look for hypoxaemia, hypercapnia, hypotension,
sepsis, stroke, seizure and its aftermath, electrolyte disturbance (sodium,
calcium), and alcohol or drug withdrawal.

# Escalation

A patient scoring 3 on consciousness has an aggregate NEWS2 of at least 3 and
should be reviewed by a competent clinician without delay. Reduced consciousness
with an unprotected airway, a falling respiratory rate, or hypoxaemia is an
emergency and needs the critical-care team. Recheck and document the ACVPU level
with every set of observations so the trend is visible.
