# Target saturations

For most acutely unwell adults, target an oxygen saturation of 94-98%. For
patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure (for example chronic
obstructive pulmonary disease, obesity hypoventilation, chest wall disease), aim
for 88-92% pending blood gas results and use a controlled delivery device.

# Responding to a falling SpO2

- **SpO2 94-95%**: recheck the probe and trace, increase observation frequency,
  and look for a cause (atelectasis, early pneumonia, fluid overload, pain
  limiting breathing).
- **SpO2 92-93%**: start or increase supplemental oxygen to reach the target
  range, sit the patient upright, and request clinical review. This corresponds
  to a NEWS2 SpO2 sub-score of 2.
- **SpO2 91% or below**: this scores 3 on NEWS2 by itself. Give oxygen
  immediately, call for urgent review, obtain an arterial or venous blood gas,
  and arrange a chest radiograph. Consider high-flow nasal oxygen or non-invasive
  ventilation if hypoxaemia persists despite standard oxygen.

# Escalation

Hypoxaemia that does not correct with 15 L/min via a reservoir mask, a rising
respiratory rate above 25, or exhaustion are indications for critical-care
assessment for respiratory support. Record the inspired oxygen with every set of
saturations so that trends are interpretable.
