# When to screen for sepsis

Screen any patient with a suspected or confirmed infection who also shows signs
of acute illness. A NEWS2 aggregate of 5 or more, or a single parameter scoring
3, in a patient with likely infection should trigger a sepsis screen. Additional
red flags include new confusion, reduced urine output, mottled or ashen skin,
non-blanching rash, and a systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg or more than
40 mmHg below the patient's normal value.

# Immediate actions: the Sepsis Six

Deliver all six within one hour of recognition:

1. Ensure a senior clinician attends.
2. Give high-flow oxygen if saturations are below target.
3. Take blood cultures and consider the source of infection.
4. Give intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics.
5. Give an intravenous fluid bolus (balanced crystalloid) and reassess.
6. Measure serial lactate; a lactate above 2 mmol/L indicates tissue
   hypoperfusion and a level above 4 mmol/L is a marker of severe illness.

# After the first hour

Reassess vital signs, mental state, urine output and lactate. Escalate to
critical care if hypotension persists after fluid resuscitation, if lactate
remains elevated, or if the patient needs vasopressors to maintain a mean
arterial pressure of 65 mmHg. Persisting hypoxaemia with an increased work of
breathing should prompt early critical-care review for respiratory support.
