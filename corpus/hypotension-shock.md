# Blood pressure thresholds on NEWS2

A systolic blood pressure of 111-219 scores 0. A systolic of 101-110 scores 1,
91-100 scores 2, and 90 or below scores 3. A reading of 220 or above also scores
3 and needs its own assessment for hypertensive emergency. Always compare against
the patient's usual blood pressure: a 40 mmHg fall from baseline is significant
even if the absolute number looks acceptable.

# Initial approach to hypotension

Assess airway, breathing and circulation. Lie the patient flat and raise the
legs. Establish large-bore intravenous access and give a balanced crystalloid
bolus of 250-500 mL, then reassess blood pressure, heart rate, capillary refill
and mental state. Identify the mechanism: hypovolaemia (bleeding, dehydration,
gastrointestinal losses), distributive (sepsis, anaphylaxis), cardiogenic
(myocardial infarction, arrhythmia), or obstructive (tension pneumothorax,
pulmonary embolism, tamponade).

# Escalation

Hypotension that does not respond to 1-2 fluid boluses, a lactate above 2
mmol/L, reduced urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour, or new confusion all indicate
the need for urgent critical-care review. Patients requiring vasopressors to keep
a mean arterial pressure of 65 mmHg need management in a critical-care
environment with continuous arterial monitoring.
