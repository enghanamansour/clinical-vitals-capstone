# Assessing a fast heart rate

A sustained pulse above 130 scores 2 on NEWS2 and above 130 with adverse
features is an emergency. Assess for adverse features: shock (systolic blood
pressure below 90, pallor, sweating, cold extremities), syncope, myocardial
ischaemia (chest pain or ischaemic changes), and heart failure (pulmonary
oedema). Any adverse feature in the presence of a tachyarrhythmia calls for
immediate senior and critical-care involvement and consideration of synchronised
cardioversion.

# Stable new atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response

If the patient is stable, obtain a 12-lead ECG, check electrolytes including
potassium and magnesium, and look for a precipitant such as sepsis, hypovolaemia,
pulmonary embolism, alcohol, or thyrotoxicosis. Treating the precipitant often
controls the rate. Rate control with a beta blocker or a rate-limiting calcium
channel blocker is usually first line; digoxin is an option in heart failure.

# Monitoring

Put the patient on continuous cardiac monitoring, repeat vital signs at least
hourly, and recalculate NEWS2 with each set. A tachycardia that rises further, or
a systolic blood pressure that falls below 100, indicates deterioration and
should be escalated urgently.
